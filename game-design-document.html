Game Design Document: Pirate Blast

Game Design · Mechanics · Level Design

1.Brief Introduction of The Game Concept

The game's main theme is seafaring, telling the story of a small seafaring crew and their adventures across a map filled with mysterious places, pirates, loots, and any other things like that. The main characters are our captain and a sailor. The captain also has a clever parrot which helps the crew in some situations. While this crew is traveling on a small ship, their ship gets damaged due to bad weather conditions, and they end up stranded on a lost island. While the captain and his compaines tries to survive on the island, they both explore the island and discover mysteries that send them on a journey. The journey that begins this way takes our crew on an adventure where they will visit many island and places.

2. Gameplay mechanics for the first 50 levels (Obstacles, Power-Ups, and Boosters)

2.1 Obstacles

In the first 50 levels of the game, there are 6 different obstacles. The obstacles are classified using the property framework defined in Section 0.2, with two additional properties introduced specifically for this game:

Fragile: The obstacle cannot be cleared by an adjacent match, but it breaks when hit by a power-up. Its intended collection method (such as reaching the bottom row by gravity) only works when the obstacle is still intact. So breaking it with a power-up means losing that collection chance.

Direction-Conditioned: The obstacle reacts only to adjacent matches made from a specific direction. Matches from any other side have no effect.

2.2 Power-Ups

The game features four different power-ups, each created by blasting a group of same-colored cubes that meets a specific size or shape condition. Power-ups spawn at the position of the tapped cube that triggered the blast, and they are activated either by tapping them directly or by being triggered through chain reactions with other matches (it can be possible just for Pufferfish) and power-ups. When two power-ups are combined, they produce stronger effects (covered in detail in the Combo section). The four power-ups can be seen in Table 3.2 below.

Name

Creation Condition

Definition

Pufferfish

4 same-colored cubes in a 2×2 square

  • The Pufferfish is a passive, color-bound power-up that the player cannot activate directly. It is created in the color of the group that formed it and stays on the grid.
  • Whenever a cube of its own color ends up adjacent to it, the Pufferfish puffs up and bursts; destroying itself, that cube, any other same colored cubes connected to that cube, and the adjacent tiles within the “+” shape.
  • The Pufferfish also reacts to obstacles: if any obstacle ends up adjacent to it, the Pufferfish bursts and deals damage to that obstacle, regardless of the obstacle's color or direction condition. However, Fragile obstacles such as the Bottle are an exception; the Pufferfish does not react to them, since breaking a Fragile obstacle would cost the player their collection chance. In this sense, the Pufferfish always acts in the player's favor.
  • If the Pufferfish is not triggered within 4 player moves after its creation, it just explodes and clear or damage 4 adjacent tile within the “+” shape.

Anchor

5 or 6 same-colored cubes in a single group

  • The Anchor is a two-directional power-up with directions pointing to opposite sides, indicating which row or column it will clear when activated.
  • Its direction is determined randomly at the moment of creation and stays fixed until activation. When triggered, it clears every item along its row or column.
  • However, if the group forming it is shaped like an “L” shape (5 or 6 cubes), the two anchor directions point at a 90 degree angle to each other, with random direction choice. For example, one arrow points left and the other points up. When activated, the Anchor clears items only along the two directions its arrows point to, producing an L-shaped sweep from its position.
  • In either case, it deals one damage to any obstacle in its path.

Barrel

7 or 8 same-colored cubes in a single group

  • When activated, the Barrel explodes outward in a diamond-shaped pattern, clearing every item up to 2 tiles away from its position in any direction. The explosion affects 13 tiles in total, forming a diamond shape rather than a square area.

Octopus

9 or more same-colored cubes in a single group

  • The Octopus is the most powerful single power-up in the game. It is created in the color of the group that formed it, and when activated, it clears every cube of its own color across the entire grid.

3. Concept Ideas for Basic Match Elements, Power-Ups, Boosters, and Obstacles

3.1Basic Match Elements.

The game features five different match items, each represented by a distinct themed object and a distinct color. Following the same design logic used in Toon Blast, the match items are differentiated not only by color but also by silhouette and icon. It will allow the player to instantly recognize each item. To remain consistent with the visual conventions of established tap-to-blast titles the match items are designed as cube shaped objects. This shape choice signals the game's tap-to-blast nature to the player from the very first screen.

4.Meta Game Design

The meta-game part of the game will designed around concepts that feel as open as possible and stay connected to each other. The player progresses through certain level sections (for example, Levels 1-10) on specific islands. When the player first arrives on an island, most of the island is hidden and covered with clouds. As the player completes levels and returns to the main menu, the hidden parts of the island slowly become visible. The player earns one star for each completed level, and uses these stars to build things along the path they walk on the island, turning that island into an area they can keep using and revisiting. While the player progresses through the levels and uncovers the mystery of the island, building things on the places they have walked through gives them a sense of conquest, which creates a feeling of satisfaction. When all the levels of an island are completed, the main characters find a mysterious chest, and items such as boosters, power-ups, and gold come out of it, along with a unique collectible item special to that island. These items are gathered in the Collectibles section of the main menu, giving the player a collector's feeling. This both creates satisfaction and lets the player revisit and inspect the collectibles they have gathered whenever they want, providing a visual satisfaction as well. 5. Level Design Strategy

When designing the levels of the game, the design should pull the player into the game as much as possible. The obstacles and grid types should be distributed across the levels in a way that creates a unique feeling in each one. Obstacles should be introduced one by one through easy levels so that the player gets used to these mechanics. As new obstacles are added, they should be combined with each other, both to give the player a greater sense of achieving something while playing and to let the player's hands get used to the mechanics. While designing these levels, rising and falling difficulty curves will be used in certain sections. As the levels progress, the game gets harder, but to prevent the player from getting frustrated, this difficulty level will increase in a wave-like pattern. While the grid types described in the framework will be used, the early levels will mostly use simple grid types so that the player gets used to the other mechanics. As different grid types are slowly shown to the player, care will be taken that these levels are not so hard.

For the first levels of the game, it will used a simple but instructive level design that we often see in other blast and match games. This will feel familiar to people who play these blast games and they will grasp the mechanic easily. While for those who do not usually play this type of game, it will be a section they can pass through without difficulty. While the box obstacle is commonly used in similar games and creates a similar look in every game, the rope obstacle used here with a very similar logic will feel visually different to the player while pulling them into the game through similar mechanic. When the player progress there will appear more complex levels with different combination of obstacles or grid types.

When each island is finished, a minigame will appear in order to visually show that the team is moving to the next island. In this minigame, player needs to open the right paths for our ship that wants to move forward through the sea current and help it find its way out. This will give the player a sense of relief and a visual satisfaction at the end of each section. The name of these levels will be "Leaving the Island" and playing them will not be mandatory.

To make the level designs easier to understand and follow, they are divided into four categories:

  • Introduction & Tutorial Levels
  • Combination Levels
  • Challenge Levels
  • Single Obstacle High Target Levels

These categories are examined below in order, with example levels shown for each. Each example level is presented with an estimated move count and goal number; however, to get a full insight into these values, the levels would need to be tested in practice. After these parts, a minigame level design will be shown.

Note: In the levels, the colored squares shown in a single solid color represent the match cubes in the game. If a grid tile is not marked with a specific color, it can be random; if a color is specified, it has been placed intentionally.

6.Home Screen UI Design and Gameplay Screen UI Design

The home screen and in-game screen were designed with the goal of creating an interface that feels as open as possible. As described in the Meta-Game Design section, the player sees the island they are currently on in the main menu and sometimes builds certain objects on it by completing tasks. For this reason, the indicators on this screen are grouped on the left side, leaving the top and right side of the screen as open as possible. So, information such as the player's profile, gold, and tasks is gathered on a single side, creating a dedicated area where the player can check their information easily. Settings button placed right of the top screen apart from these indicators.

These indicators/buttons are also placed slightly below the top edge of the screen, so that in later stages of the game a thin, long panel showing collected items from events and similar features can be added in that space. The areas are labeled with text to indicate which buttons they represent.

When the Journey button on the main menu is tapped, the player can see the islands ahead on their journey from a distance, and once an island is completed, they can tap on it to revisit that area. The wireframe UI drawings of both the menu screen and the Journey screen are shown in the Figure 3.2 below.

Figure 3.2 Home & Journey Screen UI Wireframe

The in-game screen was also designed to feel as symmetric and open as possible. The top panel contains, from left to right, a section with the character's animations, a section with the goals, and a section showing the move count. The goals section is placed in the middle of the panel because the focus should be drawn to how many goals need to be collected while playing. The character animation panel placed on its left supports the gameplay with animations that make it more enjoyable. Under the moves section, in addition to the remaining move count, the player's current level and the number of lives they have are also shown. This helps the player get a better sense of whether they should take a risk, use a booster, or get an extra moves on a level they're struggling with. The wireframe UI drawing of the in-game screen can be seen in Figure 3.4 below.

Figure 3.3 In-Game Screen UI Wireframe

The game area (grid) is placed exactly in the middle of the scene to keep the player's focus on the gameplay. At the bottom of the screen, there are four booster buttons and a settings button. The booster buttons are designed in a rectangular shape while the settings button is designed in a round shape, making them easy to tell apart. Also, no background panel was added behind the buttons in this bottom section, so the main game screen doesn't feel crowded.
